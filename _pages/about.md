I am a PhD Candidate in applied mathematics at the Courant Institute, New York University, advised by [Georg Stadler](https://math.nyu.edu/~stadler/). My research focuses on uncertainty quantification, particularly Bayesian inverse problems that arise from PDE-governed systems. These are parameter estimation problems that are often infinite-dimensional, so much of my research is aimed at deriving methods that are computationally feasible even in very high dimensions. 

My current research pursues three directions. The first develops efficient methods for marginalizing unknown hyperparameters in PDE-governed Bayesian inverse problems, and is available as an [arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03355). The second extends this framework to optimal experimental design, asking how to optimally place sensors to maximize information gain in the presence of hyperparameter uncertainty. The third explores the relationship between the PDE-governed framework and INLA, a widely-used approach in geostatistics that addresses strikingly similar problems from a different perspective.

The first four years of my PhD were supported by the Department of Energy's [Computational Sciences Graduate Fellowship](https://www.krellinst.org/csgf/). Prior to that, I completed my B.S. in Mathematics with Computer Science at MIT. I expect to finish my PhD in Spring 2027, and I am on the academic job market this fall.
